The uncertainty principle was given by: | Werner Heisenberg | de Broglie | Schrodinger | Bohr | Position and momentum cannot both be known exactly.

The electronic configuration of copper (Z = 29) is: | [Ar] 3d10 4s1 | [Ar] 3d9 4s2 | [Ar] 3d8 4s2 4p1 | [Ar] 4s2 3d9 | Fully filled d subshell is extra stable.

Hund's rule says electrons in orbitals of equal energy: | Remain unpaired as far as possible | Pair up first | Go to higher shells | Have opposite spins always | Pairing starts only after each orbital has one electron.

The modern periodic law is based on: | Atomic number | Atomic mass | Neutron number | Density | It was based on Moseley's work.
Eka-aluminium predicted by Mendeleev is now called: | Gallium | Germanium | Scandium | Aluminium | Eka-silicon is germanium.
The modern periodic table has: | 18 groups and 7 periods | 8 groups and 7 periods | 18 groups and 9 periods | 16 groups | Lanthanoids and actinoids are placed separately.
Elements of group 1 are called: | Alkali metals | Alkaline earth metals | Halogens | Noble gases | They have ns1 configuration.
Elements of group 17 are called: | Halogens | Chalcogens | Pnictogens | Noble gases | They have ns2 np5 configuration.
The f-block elements are also called: | Inner transition elements | Transition elements | Representative elements | Noble gases | They include lanthanoids and actinoids.
The IUPAC name of element 104 under temporary nomenclature is: | Unnilquadium | Unnilunium | Ununbium | Unnilpentium | 1 = un, 0 = nil, 4 = quad.
Atomic radius decreases across a period because: | Effective nuclear charge increases | Shells increase | Electrons are removed | Neutrons increase | Electrons are added to the same shell.
Which has the largest atomic radius? | Cs | Li | Na | K | Size increases down a group.
The element with the highest electronegativity is: | Fluorine | Oxygen | Chlorine | Neon | Pauling value about 4.0.
First ionisation enthalpy of N is higher than O because: | N has a half-filled stable p subshell | O is smaller | N has more protons | O has fewer electrons | Removing an electron from O gives a half-filled p subshell.
Noble gases have large positive electron gain enthalpy because: | They have stable full shells | They are small | They are metals | They have low ionisation enthalpy | Adding an electron requires a new shell.
Which ion is the smallest? | Mg2+ | Na+ | F- | O2- | All are isoelectronic; Mg2+ has the most protons.
The most metallic element in period 3 is: | Sodium | Magnesium | Aluminium | Silicon | Metallic character decreases across a period.
The oxide of sodium is: | Basic | Acidic | Amphoteric | Neutral | Na2O forms NaOH in water.
Al2O3 is: | Amphoteric | Basic | Acidic | Neutral | It reacts with both acids and bases.
Elements in the same group have similar chemical properties because they have the same: | Valence shell configuration | Atomic mass | Number of shells | Atomic radius | Group members share outer electron arrangement.
# Chemical Bonding and Molecular Structure
The Kossel-Lewis approach explains bonding using the: | Octet rule | Hybridisation | MO theory | VSEPR only | Atoms gain, lose or share electrons to complete octets.
Formal charge on oxygen in O3 at the central atom is: | +1 | -1 | 0 | +2 | The central O has three bonds and one lone pair.
BF3 is an example of: | Incomplete octet | Expanded octet | Odd electron molecule | Ionic compound | Boron has only six electrons.
NO and NO2 are examples of: | Odd electron molecules | Expanded octets | Incomplete octets | Ionic compounds | They have unpaired electrons.
PCl5 and SF6 show: | Expanded octets | Incomplete octets | Odd electrons | No covalent bonds | Central atoms use d orbitals in older models.
Lattice enthalpy is the energy needed to: | Separate one mole of an ionic solid into gaseous ions | Melt a solid | Break a covalent bond | Ionise a gas | Higher lattice enthalpy means a more stable solid.
Bond order of N2 is: | 3 | 2 | 1 | 2.5 | N2 has a triple bond.
Bond order of O2 is: | 2 | 3 | 1 | 1.5 | O2 has a double bond and two unpaired electrons.
O2 is paramagnetic because it has: | Two unpaired electrons in antibonding orbitals | No unpaired electrons | A triple bond | Ionic bonds | MO theory explains this.
He2 does not exist because its bond order is: | 0 | 1 | 2 | 0.5 | Bonding and antibonding electrons cancel.
The shape of SF6 is: | Octahedral | Tetrahedral | Square planar | Trigonal bipyramidal | Six bond pairs, no lone pairs.
The shape of XeF4 is: | Square planar | Tetrahedral | Octahedral | See-saw | Four bond pairs and two lone pairs.
The shape of SF4 is: | See-saw | Tetrahedral | Square planar | Octahedral | Four bond pairs and one lone pair.
The shape of ClF3 is: | T-shaped | Trigonal planar | Pyramidal | Linear | Three bond pairs and two lone pairs.
The shape of H2O is: | Bent | Linear | Tetrahedral | Pyramidal | Two lone pairs push the bonds closer.
The hybridisation of carbon in ethene is: | sp2 | sp3 | sp | dsp2 | Each carbon forms three sigma bonds.
Which molecule has zero dipole moment? | CO2 | H2O | NH3 | HCl | Bond dipoles cancel in the linear molecule.
NH3 has a higher dipole moment than NF3 because: | Lone pair and bond dipoles add in NH3 but oppose in NF3 | N-F bonds are non-polar | NF3 is ionic | NH3 is planar | Both are pyramidal.
Intramolecular hydrogen bonding is found in: | o-Nitrophenol | p-Nitrophenol | Water | HF | It lowers boiling point and water solubility.
Ice is less dense than water because of: | Open hydrogen-bonded structure | Ionic bonds | Covalent bonds | Van der Waals forces only | Each water molecule bonds to four others in ice.
According to Fajans' rules, covalent character increases when: | The cation is small and highly charged | The cation is large | The anion is small | Charges are low | Small cations polarise large anions more.
A sigma bond is formed by: | Head-on overlap of orbitals | Sideways overlap | Transfer of electrons | Hydrogen bonding | It is stronger than a pi bond.
# Thermodynamics
A system that can exchange energy but not matter is: | Closed | Open | Isolated | Adiabatic | A sealed flask is a closed system.
A thermos flask approximates an: | Isolated system | Open system | Closed system | Living system | No exchange of matter or energy.
Which is an extensive property? | Volume | Temperature | Density | Pressure | Extensive properties depend on amount.
Which is an intensive property? | Molar heat capacity | Mass | Volume | Internal energy | It does not depend on amount.
Which is a state function? | Enthalpy | Work | Heat | Path length | State functions depend only on initial and final states.
For an isolated system, change in internal energy is: | Zero | Positive | Negative | Infinite | No heat or work crosses its boundary.
The first law of thermodynamics is: | Delta U = q + w | Delta U = q - T | Delta H = q only | Delta S = q/T | IUPAC sign convention for work done on the system.
For an ideal gas undergoing isothermal reversible expansion, Delta U is: | Zero | Positive | Negative | Equal to q only if q is zero | Internal energy of an ideal gas depends only on temperature.
Free expansion of an ideal gas into vacuum involves work: | Equal to zero | Positive | Negative | Infinite | There is no external pressure.
Delta H - Delta U equals: | Delta n_g RT | nRT | Delta n_g / RT | Zero always | For reactions involving gases.
Standard enthalpy of formation of an element in its reference state is: | Zero | Positive | Negative | One | For example, O2 gas or graphite.
Hess's law is a consequence of enthalpy being a: | State function | Path function | Extensive property only | Intensive property | Total enthalpy change does not depend on the path.
Combustion reactions are: | Exothermic | Endothermic | Neither | Always non-spontaneous | Heat is released.
Entropy is a measure of: | Disorder or randomness | Heat content | Work done | Pressure | Gases have higher entropy than liquids.
Entropy increases when: | A solid melts | A gas condenses | A liquid freezes | Temperature drops | Particles become more disordered.
Delta G = Delta H - T Delta S. A reaction is spontaneous when Delta G is: | Negative | Positive | Zero | Infinite | At constant T and P.
A reaction with Delta H positive and Delta S positive is spontaneous at: | High temperature | Low temperature | All temperatures | No temperature | T Delta S must exceed Delta H.
At equilibrium, Delta G is: | Zero | Negative | Positive | Equal to Delta H | The forward and backward tendencies balance.
Relation between Delta G standard and K is: | Delta G standard = -RT ln K | Delta G = RT ln K | Delta G = K/RT | Delta G = -K | A large K means a negative standard Delta G.
Enthalpy of neutralisation of a strong acid by a strong base is about: | -57.1 kJ/mol | -13.6 kJ/mol | -100 kJ/mol | +57.1 kJ/mol | It is the formation of water from H+ and OH-.
