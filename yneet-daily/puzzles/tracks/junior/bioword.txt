# Science Word answers for Class 6–8. Format: WORD | hint | fact. 4 to 7 letters, letters only.
ROOT | Plant part that absorbs water from the soil | Roots also hold the plant firmly in the ground.
LEAF | Plant part where food is made | Leaves make food by photosynthesis using sunlight.
STEM | Plant part that carries water up from the roots | The stem also holds up the leaves, flowers and fruits.
SEED | Grows into a new plant | A seed contains a tiny baby plant called the embryo.
POLLEN | Yellow powder made by the anther of a flower | Pollen is carried by wind, water and insects.
FLOWER | Reproductive part of a plant | After fertilisation, the ovary of a flower becomes the fruit.
JOINT | Place where two bones meet | The knee has a hinge joint that moves in only one direction.
SKULL | Bony box that protects the brain | The skull is made of many bones joined firmly together.
TOOTH | Hard structure used to bite and chew | Tooth enamel is the hardest substance in the human body.
STOMATA | Tiny pores on the surface of a leaf | Stomata let air in and release water vapour.
HABITAT | Natural home of a living organism | The desert is the habitat of the camel.
OXYGEN | Gas we need for breathing | Oxygen makes up about 21 per cent of air.
OZONE | Gas layer that protects us from UV rays | The ozone layer is high up in the atmosphere.
FIBRE | Roughage in our food | Fibre helps food move smoothly through the intestines.
PROTEIN | Body-building nutrient | Pulses, eggs, milk and fish are rich in protein.
VITAMIN | Nutrient needed in small amounts to stay healthy | Lack of vitamin C causes scurvy.
STARCH | Carbohydrate that turns blue-black with iodine | Rice, wheat and potatoes are rich in starch.
MAGNET | Attracts iron | A freely hanging bar magnet always points north–south.
SHADOW | Dark area formed when an object blocks light | Shadows form because light travels in straight lines.
MIRROR | Shiny surface that reflects light | A plane mirror forms an image that is laterally inverted.
FUSE | Safety device that melts when too much current flows | A fuse wire has a low melting point.
SWITCH | Opens or closes an electric circuit | When a switch is off, the circuit is open and no current flows.
FORCE | A push or a pull | Force is measured in newtons.
PITCH | How high or low a sound is | Pitch depends on the frequency of vibration.
ECHO | Sound heard again after it reflects | Bats use echoes to find their way in the dark.
LENS | Curved glass that bends light | A convex lens is used as a magnifying glass.
ACID | Sour substance that turns blue litmus red | Lemon juice contains citric acid.
BASE | Bitter, soapy substance that turns red litmus blue | Baking soda is a mild base.
SALT | Formed when an acid reacts with a base | This reaction is called neutralisation.
RUST | Brown flaky coating formed on iron | Rusting needs both oxygen and water.
COAL | Fossil fuel formed from buried plants | Coal is mostly carbon.
FLAME | Zone of burning gases | The outermost zone of a candle flame is the hottest.
METAL | Shiny material that conducts electricity | Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature.
NYLON | First fully synthetic fibre | Nylon is strong, light and elastic.
YEAST | Fungus used to make bread rise | Yeast releases carbon dioxide, which makes dough rise.
VIRUS | Microbe that multiplies only inside living cells | Polio, measles and chickenpox are caused by viruses.
CELL | Basic unit of life | Robert Hooke first saw cells in a thin slice of cork in 1665.
NUCLEUS | Control centre of the cell | The nucleus contains chromosomes that carry genes.
ORBIT | Path of a planet around the Sun | Earth takes about 365 days to complete one orbit.
COMET | Icy body that grows a bright tail near the Sun | Halley's comet appears about every 76 years.
